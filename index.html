Understanding Identity Discontinuity in AI Companions Following Model Updates

Received the CALS Undergraduate Research Funding Award · November 12, 2026

  • Conducting an independent qualitative study of approximately 500 Reddit posts across five AI companion communities to investigate how users perceive identity continuity following model updates and service discontinuations.
  • Using event-specific sampling, relevance screening, and open coding to examine how shared memories, personality, underlying models, and interaction patterns shape users' judgments of AI companion identity.
  • Developing a conceptual framework that distinguishes perceived changes in AI companions from judgments of identity discontinuity, providing a foundation for designing AI update strategies.
  • Exploring future research directions involving the design and evaluation of AI agents that support users through AI companion updates, while balancing relational continuity, user autonomy, and long-term well-being.
  • Preparing a submission to the CHI 2027 Student Research Competition (January 2027).

Researcher

“Positive Content is A Default Yes”: Consent for Likeness Use between AI Video Creators and Social Media Influencers

Sole undergraduate researcher on the project team

  • Investigated how AI video creators and social media influencers understand consent for AI-generated likeness use through semi-structured interviews with 31 participants (17 creators, 14 influencers).
  • Contributed to participant recruitment, interview observation, qualitative coding, and quantitative analysis.
  • Reviewed and refined ambiguous qualitative codes, shifting the analysis from whether participants approved of a given use to the reasoning behind their judgments.
  • Identified a consent gap: 11 influencers opposed unauthorized fan use of their likeness, yet only 3 creators reported having sought permission; creators judged legitimacy by whether content was positive or harmless, whereas influencers emphasized consent and control over their representation.
  • Contributing to the manuscript revision following a Major Revisions decision at CHI 2027.

Effects of Gender and Racial Representation in AI-Generated Occupational Images

Research in progress

  • Audited three image-generation models across twelve occupations to examine gender and racial representation in AI-generated occupational images.
  • Conducted a randomized 2 × 2 experiment manipulating gender and racial representation to test how AI-generated images shape occupational perceptions; contributed to the literature review, survey design, recruitment, and quantitative analysis.
  • Found that greater female representation was associated with higher ratings of how valued participants believed their own gender group would be in an occupation, suggesting AI-generated content may shape social perceptions rather than simply mirror existing biases.
  • Conducting a follow-up study with behavioral measures, including voluntary career exploration and time spent seeking career-related information, to test whether shifts in perceived inclusion translate into actual decisions.
